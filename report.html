We estimated the weekly asking rent for all 65,777 City of Sydney flats, with and without parking, as at 31 Jul 2026. This page shows what we learned first, then how we did it.

  1. Local median. Median rent for the same suburb, bedrooms and parking over the last 12 months. The tidy $100 is real: most adverts and medians sit on round numbers, so 190 of 2,635 errors are exactly $100, and the median lands on one of them.
  2. Each flat's last rent. Use the flat's own last advert, or its building's, instead of a local median. Stale, so it ran $50 low.
  3. Rent index. Bring that old rent up to date using how repeat leases of the same flats moved.
  4. Leak fix. We found the input was showing us each test advert's own parking and bedrooms, and removed that. The Fold A score got slightly worse, and the result became believable.
  5. LightGBM model. Learn how far to trust each anchor, using rolling two-month training windows.
  6. Cleaner inputs. Drop date fields that looked nothing like the flats we predict, and predict the ratio to the anchor.
  7. Raw last rent. Show the model the flat's last rent as advertised, because many re-lets return at exactly the old rent. Add 5-seed averaging. Fold B improved by about $2, Fold A barely moved.
  8. Model zoo. Blend nine tree, linear, neighbour and neural models, with weights chosen only on earlier windows, and add more flat-history features.
  9. Round to $10. Quote rents in $10 steps, the way adverts are written. The median error rose to $40, but more adverts landed within $100 and bias fell to zero. This is the submitted file.

1. Why a naive estimate runs low

A naive estimate copies the last rent seen for a flat or its neighbours. That rent may be months old. When rents are rising, an old rent is a low rent, and the older it is the lower it runs.

Before the index, a typical estimate ran $50 a week low. After moving old rents forward to the target date with the index, the typical gap was +$3.62 a week.

One surprising finding

A flat's own old rent beats everything else, even when it is stale. The typical own rent we used was about 20 months old, yet once indexed it missed by about $41 on Fold A, against $100 for a recent suburb, bedrooms and parking median. The second surprise was in the supplied input: its parking and bedrooms track each flat's latest advert, so a simple date split quietly shows a model the answer. We rebuilt those fields as known at each cutoff.

Method

A blend of nine tree, linear, neighbour and neural models predicts the ratio of rent to the best past-rent anchor, with blend weights chosen on earlier windows and quotes rounded to $10. Feature importance is measured on the LightGBM member.

  1. Find the most specific past rent for each flat: its own, then its building, then its block.
  2. Move that rent forward to the target date with a rent index, so an old rent is not read as a current one.
  3. Train on rolling past dates. Each training row sees only what was known two months earlier, never its own advert.
  4. Predict twice for each flat, once with a car space and once without.

How the dates are split

The left bar in each row is what the model may learn from. The right bar is what it is tested on and never sees. Final estimates use data up to 31 Jul 2026 and aim at 31 Aug 2026, the middle of the scoring window.

What worked

  • Indexing each flat's own last rent to the target date. This took the Fold A anchor bias from -$50 to +$3.62.
  • Rolling two-month training windows, so each training row faces the same gap as the real task.
  • Rebuilding parking and bedrooms as known at each cutoff. This removed a hidden leak and created 85 Fold B adverts that test the parking premium directly.
  • Noticing how landlords behave. About a quarter of re-lets within two years return at exactly the old rent, which an index cannot show. Adding the raw last rent improved Fold B by about $2 and flats with history most of all, while Fold A barely moved.
  • Blending. A nine-model blend reached $38.02 on Fold A before rounding, against $40.25 for LightGBM alone. Weights were chosen on earlier windows, and Fold A was read once for this blend.

What didn't

  • Projecting the rent trend forward. Tuning on Fold B chose to hold the index flat at the cutoff.
  • Separating the two model targets. Log(rent / anchor) beat rent by about $1 on Fold B, and the winner changed when we removed features, so treat that choice as close to noise.
  • Public data. Thirteen sets of open-data features (G-NAF, OpenStreetMap, Census, cadastre, transport and amenities) gave no gain overall on Fold B. The best set helped first-time flats by perhaps $1 to $2 but cost flats with history about $1, and its first result did not repeat on fresh seeds.
  • Microburbs API data. Extra adverts, transport, schools, amenities, risks, noise and market series all missed our bar of $1 with a clear interval on Fold B. Market series came closest, at -$0.66. Crime was left out because its inputs are undated. Sale history was only 26% pulled, so it is untested.
  • Using the model's own parking gap. It came out around $17 to $22, compressed because anchors stay fixed between passes. We use the within-building premium, median $36. The two could not be separated on 85 Fold B adverts whose parking changed.
  • Model tuning. Hyperparameters, CatBoost, blends and separate models for first-time flats all stayed within noise.
  • Any single bigger model. TabPFN, TabICL, TabM and RealMLP gave no reliable win over plain trees on all rows. The gain came from blending many different models instead.

Limitations

  • These are asking rents from adverts, not the rent finally agreed.
  • Flats that are advertised often are better covered than flats that rarely change hands.
  • The parking and floor premiums are patterns in adverts. Other differences between flats may be mixed in.
  • Estimates describe a typical flat of that kind. Renovations, views and condition are mostly invisible to the data.
  • The test periods cover four months of 2026, so a sudden market change could behave differently.
  • Our folds may flatter us. Flats with no earlier advert keep input fields that may come from the test advert itself, and fold adverts number about 1,300 a month against 1,699 scored over two months, so the scored adverts may be a different mix.
  • Fold A was not perfectly untouched. No setting was tuned on it, but the check that found the input leak looked at its adverts, and it was read again for the final LightGBM and for the blend.
  • We have no floor area or building age, so bigger or newer flats that have parking can inflate the premium. Treat it as an upper estimate. The two premium methods tied on median error on Fold B.
  • Microburbs' $86 was measured on the frozen August and September adverts, not on our folds, so the comparison is indicative only.
  • Rounding to $10 means errors fall on $10 steps, so the median can jump in steps on the real adverts. Fold B's blend weights also saw Fold B, so its $39.87 is not a clean test.
